# Recent statistics rebuilds the vignette benchmark's foundations

The research most useful to this benchmark does not let a cheap LLM panel replace people. It offers design-based ways to make the panel's numbers answer to a small human sample, and to turn every soft judgment in the benchmark (auditors' confound grades, originality grades, authors' size bins) into a prediction scored against something measured. Five toolkits do the work. **Prediction-powered inference (PPI) and its 2024–2026 descendants** let the panel stretch human data for aggregates over pairs, by at most n/(1 − ρ²), but they cannot sharpen a single pair's human mean, and a 2025 bound says a judge no better than the models it evaluates cannot save more than half the human labels. **Cumulative-probit models with judge-specific thresholds** put human and model gaps on one latent scale, expose the ceiling that makes the intention and knowledge pairs uninformative, and give the pilot's +0.44-point same-model effect a proper place in the model. **"Varies only one factor" has a precise reading**: the version affects judgments only through the perceived factor, an exclusion restriction that placebo questions can falsify. Kneer and Machery's moral-luck study, read at source, shows this working: four knowledge and belief items barely moved (|d| ≤ 0.09) while perceived risk moved (d = 0.41) and carried the effect. **Authors' forecasts should be probabilities or intervals scored with strictly proper rules** (ranked probability score, CRPS, interval score), reported as skill over a factor baseline. They should be classified at one pre-set sample size or under confidence sequences, with a three-way verdict whose "equivalent" branch is out of reach below about 50 people per version, and with hierarchical shrinkage across pairs. **Originality is best measured on the pool**, as Hill numbers or Vendi scores over a structural partition and against a fixed reference corpus, because 2025–2026 work shows same-family models converge and that headline convergence figures depend heavily on the null and the embedding. Most 2025–2026 sources were seen only as abstracts or search extracts, so the formulas the recommendations rest on come from code read at source or were derived here. Across all five areas the binding constraint is the same: the number of distinct pairs, and τ, the pair-level disagreement between the calibrated panel and people. The first human run should be designed to estimate τ.

*Evidence tags.* Every citation carries one of four tags. **Read at source**: a researcher or the coordinator read the page, code or article itself. **Abstract checked**: the coordinator verified the arXiv ID, title, authors and abstract (all 208 IDs, 18 November 2026). The tag covers a paper's headline claim only; figures beyond the abstract remain search extracts unless the coordinator listed them as confirmed. **Search extract**: taken from a search-engine summary and not verified; read it as a lead, not a fact. **Computed**: derived or simulated within this project, either in the coordinator's [mathematical analysis](Mathematical%20analysis%20of%20the%20benchmark.md) (cited below as "analysis §n") or by the researchers; not taken from a source.

## The panel stretches human data across pairs, never within one

The human run is small, arrives a few people at a time, and is meant to show how far the cheap model panel can stand in for people. The analysis showed that the original agreement bar mostly measures human sampling noise (§4) and that the panel can help in two distinct ways (§9). The literature since 2023 explains why and supplies the estimators. PPI estimates a quantity from cheap predictions on every unit, then corrects it by the average human-minus-prediction difference on a random subset with human labels. The reference implementation computes the PPI++ mean as λ·mean(f, unlabelled) + mean(Y − λf, labelled). λ is tuned from the data and clipped to [0, 1], and the standard error adds the imputed and rectifier variances ([ppi.py](https://github.com/aangelopoulos/ppi_py/blob/main/ppi_py/ppi.py), read at source). With the optimal λ the variance is σ²[(1 − ρ²)/n + ρ²/(n + N)], where ρ is the prediction–label correlation, n the number of labelled units and N the number of unlabelled ones (computed). That formula is behind the analysis's table of what each human-rated pair is worth. PPI++ adapts to the quality of the predictions, and its intervals improve on human-only ones ([Angelopoulos, Duchi & Zrnic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01453), abstract checked). **The correction absorbs any additive or multiplicative bias in the panel; only correlation buys precision.**

**A panel that gives one number per version cannot narrow a single pair's human mean at all.** Within a version the prediction is constant, so its correlation with individual ratings is zero and the PPI estimate reduces exactly to the plain human mean (computed). This is the mistake the analysis corrected in §9, and it settles how the panel should be used. For aggregates (an author's mean score, a factor's mean gap, the share of pairs that work), the pair is the unit. The panel's gap d_p is the prediction and the human gap Δ̂_p is the label, and because Δ̂_p is unbiased for the true gap, any score linear in the gap stays unbiased (computed). For a single pair, the calibrated panel can only enter as a prior. In a normal–normal model the shrunken gap is Δ̃_p = w·Δ̂_p + (1 − w)·(α̂ + β̂·d_p + γ̂·self_p + factor term), with w = τ²/(τ² + s_p²). That prior is worth 4σ²/τ² extra participants, where τ is the scatter of true human gaps around the calibrated panel (computed; analysis §9 gives 36 participants at τ = 0.5 and 9 at τ = 1). Prediction-powered adaptive shrinkage is the published form of this idea. It debiases each problem's predictions, then shrinks towards them by an amount chosen to minimise an unbiased risk estimate ([Li & Ignatiadis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14166), abstract checked). A 2026 multi-task PPI paper argues that affine recalibrations across tasks are "oracle-equivalent" to the raw proxy for a mean ([Emmenegger, Stahler & Podimata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29249), search extract). If that holds, the panel's fitted slope and offset matter for per-pair shrinkage and not for aggregate means. The cheapest variance reduction needs no model at all. Each participant rates one version of every pair, and the mirror-form design randomises versions independently across pairs, so a participant random intercept is a valid covariate. On the actual form design, simulation gives standard errors 0.85–0.89 times the raw ones at an intraclass correlation of 0.28 (computed). That agrees with the analysis's estimate of about 11% (§3).

Two recent results cap what the panel is worth, and both should temper the right-hand end of the analysis's §9 table. Dorner, Nastl and Hardt show that when a judge is no more accurate than the model it evaluates, no debiasing method can save more than half the ground-truth labels ([LLM as judge won't beat twice the data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13341), abstract checked). In the PPI++ formula a twofold saving corresponds to ρ² = 0.5, or ρ ≈ 0.71 (computed). The analogy here is loose, since the panel predicts human gaps rather than grading answers. But judges and authors come from one model family, so the honest planning figure is **at most a twofold saving on author-level aggregates** until the human run shows a pair-level ρ well above 0.7. The analysis's 5.3 at ρ = 0.9 is a figure to be earned, not assumed. Li, Yu and Li find that a fixed panel of 32 LLM judges, matched against human label distributions on ChaosNLI, is worth about **2.3–6.5 humans** depending on the matching criterion, and about 2 under a binary-error diagnostic ([How many humans are 32 LLM judges worth?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21277), abstract checked; measured on NLI items, not vignettes). The pilot panel is smaller than it looks. Four judge models with five near-identical repeats have a design effect of about five, so twenty ratings per version carry roughly the information of four raters (computed). Uncertainty should be computed with the judge model as the unit, not the run.

The panel's gaps should be expected to be too large, not merely noisy. Hewitt and colleagues report that GPT-4's predictions for 70 preregistered survey experiments correlated r = 0.85 with the observed effects and got 90% of directions right, but **systematically overestimated effect sizes** ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10742-x), search extract). Li and Ji find that LLM simulators which reproduce descriptive means and distributions still estimate treatment effects poorly ([When simulations look right but causal effects go wrong](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02458), abstract checked). Bisbee and colleagues report that synthetic survey answers match human averages but carry less variation ([Political Analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/B92267DC26195C7F36E63EA04A47D2FE), search extract). Together these explain why "same verdict on 24 of 30 pairs" becomes unattainable if human gaps are about half the panel's (§4). They also support the analysis's replacement for that bar. Estimate the slope of human gaps on panel gaps and rescale by it; do not expect the size bins to agree; and never standardise panel gaps by the panel's own compressed spread.

Self-preference makes the panel's error correlated with the author. That is exactly the case in which naive pooling of panel and human data misleads. Design-based supervised learning gives valid inference "even when surrogates are arbitrarily biased", provided each unit's probability of receiving a gold-standard label is known ([Egami, Hinck, Stewart & Wei](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04746), abstract checked). Active statistical inference concentrates labels where the model is uncertain and contains PPI as the uniform-sampling case ([Zrnic & Candès](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03208), abstract checked). Stratified PPI gains when autorater accuracy varies across subdomains ([Fisch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04291), abstract checked). For this benchmark, choose which future pairs get people with recorded probabilities π_p. Make π_p larger where the calibrated panel is expected to be most wrong: π_p proportional to the root expected squared residual is the Neyman-type rule (computed; still to be checked against the active-inference paper). Floor it at 0.1–0.2, stratify by author so author contrasts remain estimable, and pass the weights to `ppi_py` through its per-unit weight arguments (read at source). Four signals predict a large residual: disagreement between judge models, the self-minus-other gap, the size of the panel gap, and closeness to a bin edge. Each participant rates all thirty pairs, so ratings cluster by person. Broska and Howes show that ignoring within-cluster dependence makes PPI intervals under-cover, and they give closed-form cluster-robust PPI++ standard errors ([Cluster-robust PPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09601), abstract checked). Closed-form power and sample-size formulas for PPI now exist ([Power analysis for PPI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16041), abstract checked). A 2026 two-stage design paper reports that stratifying human review by how predictable the LLM is cut the human budget by up to 12.9% ([Kim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16354), search extract).

People arrive one at a time, so each recalibration is itself a sequential look. Prediction-powered confidence sequences that stay valid as labelled and unlabelled data grow have been proposed ([Kilian, Cortinovis & Caron, NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/118778), search extract); the simpler alternative is to fix the calibration point in advance. The current survey is phase 1. Every pair gets people, so no PPI is needed for per-pair estimates, and the survey's job is to estimate the calibration. A leave-one-pair-out weighted regression of human gaps on each judge model's gap, with a self indicator and factor terms, estimates four things (computed design): the inflation slope, γ (self-preference in scale points), judge-specific slopes and τ². The five null controls estimate the panel's spurious-effect rate. The five classics have no author, so they identify the slope free of self-preference. Human noise attenuates the observed panel–human correlation by the square root of the reliability of the human gaps. With thirty pairs its standard error near ρ = 0.8 is about 0.07 (computed), so the analysis's bar of a noise-corrected correlation of at least 0.8 should be reported with its interval. In phase 2 the panel judges most new pairs. Aggregates then use pair-level PPI++ with author strata, recorded π and cluster-robust errors; per-pair estimates use empirical-Bayes shrinkage. Any author ranking should use prediction-powered rank-sets, because rank-sets built from LLM comparisons alone were often inconsistent with human preferences ([Chatzi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17826), abstract checked). The social-science frameworks agree on the boundary: results from LLMs alone are exploratory, and confirmatory claims need a human validation sample ([Hullman et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15785); [Ludwig, Mullainathan & Rambachan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07031), both abstract checked). To catch calibration drift, keep a standing random human sample every round and treat any new author family as a new stratum.

## One latent ordinal scale absorbs ceilings, judge habits and self-preference

Treating 1–7 ratings as numbers on an interval scale is the root of several pilot symptoms. Liddell and Kruschke show that metric models of ordinal data can distort effect sizes and even reverse the ordering of latent means; they recommend ordered-probit models ([JESP 2018](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/9bcd0f5e-7837-4f9c-ac07-a114e595e146), search extract). A counterpoint limits the scope. Simulations of t-tests on binned Likert data found acceptable coverage and type I error even with floor effects ([Value in Health](https://www.ispor.org/publications/journals/value-in-health/abstract/Volume-23--Issue-5/Investigation-Into-the-Effects-of-Using-Normal-Distribution-Theory-Methodology-for-Likert-Scale-Patient-Reported-Outcome-Data-From-Varying-Underlying-Distributions-Including-Floor-Ceiling-Effects), search extract). So mean-based tests remain adequate for asking *whether* an effect exists. The damage falls on effect sizes and on comparisons across conditions, which is what the benchmark scores. The analysis has already quantified the mechanism: under an ordered-probit reading, a one-point latent shift appears as a 0.94-point gap mid-scale but only 0.50 near 6.5 (§1, computed). The brms documentation defines the cumulative family as P(y = k) = g(τ_{k+1} − η) − g(τ_k − η), with thresholds as the intercepts and optional category-specific effects ([brms families vignette](https://github.com/paul-buerkner/brms/blob/master/vignettes/brms_families.Rmd), read at source). Bürkner and Vuorre's tutorial covers fitting such models in psychology ([AMPPS 2019](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/ampps/2515245918823199/), search extract).

The backbone model for the benchmark treats every individual rating as an ordinal outcome: P(y ≤ k) = Φ(τ_{k,m} − η), with η = δ_p·v + u_p + u_m + u_{m,p} + b_m·v and δ_p = μ_f + α_a + (αφ)_{a,f} + e_p (computed specification). Here v = ±½ codes the version, m the judge, a the author and f the factor. Judge-specific thresholds τ_{k,m} absorb each judge model's scale use, the ordinal form of a rater-severity facet. The judge slope b_m lets judges differ in how sharply they separate versions. Repeats enter through the u_m and u_{m,p} terms rather than counting as independent, which removes the near-zero within-cell SD behind the pilot's inflated standardised effects. Report δ_p on the latent scale, and convert it back to an expected raw gap for a fixed reference panel by posterior prediction. Cohen's d computed on within-cell SD should be retired. When ratings pile into the top category, that category is effectively censored, so a ceiling factor shows up as a wide posterior with between-author differences indistinguishable from zero. That is the formal version of the analysis's zero-entropy finding for the intention and knowledge factors. The fitting syntax (brms `cumulative("probit")` with `thres(gr = judge)`, or `ordinal::clmm`) comes from the researchers' own knowledge and needs checking against current documentation.

The same model, fitted to people and the panel together, puts the two on one scale. Thresholds and latent spread are specific to each panel, and the human latent SD is fixed at 1. Whether δ_p can be shared between panels is then the ordinal analogue of testing metric and then scalar measurement invariance (computed design). The evidence says not to assume invariance. A 2023 study found a standard personality inventory is not measurement-invariant between humans and LLMs ([Challenging the validity of personality tests for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05297), abstract checked). A 2026 audit describes LLM respondents as over-coherent and low in variance, and argues that validation without human baselines overstates fidelity ([Plausible but not valid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14606), abstract checked). Shape fails as well as spread: one 2026 paper reports that LLM item skewness correlated .397 with human skewness, against a benchmark of .754 ([Item-mean surrogates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29455), search extract). For each version, compare the human and panel rating histograms with Jensen–Shannon or Wasserstein distance, not only their means. The panel's spread can be raised by sampling temperature or persona variation, and one 2026 paper restores realistic variance by calibrating a single temperature parameter ([Correcting mode collapse in silicon sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28550), abstract checked). Matching the spread does not guarantee matching shape or joint structure, though, so check it on held-out pairs.

The pilot's same-model bonus belongs inside this model, not beside it. Panickssery, Bowman and Feng show that LLM evaluators recognise and favour their own generations, and that self-recognition ability tracks the strength of self-preference ([LLM evaluators recognize and favor their own generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076), abstract checked). Wataoka and colleagues attribute the bias to familiarity: lower perplexity of the text to the evaluator ([Self-preference bias in LLM-as-a-judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819), abstract checked). The 2026 counterweight is "Are LLM evaluators really narcissists?". It argues that apparent self-preference can reflect the evaluator's own failures on items it gets wrong, and corrects for this with an evaluator-quality baseline ([arXiv 2601.22548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22548), abstract checked). Its widely quoted figure, that this explains 89.6% of measured self-preference, is a search extract only. Panels of smaller models from disjoint families show less intra-model bias than a single large judge and cost far less ([Verga et al., PoLL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796), abstract checked). Generating long reasoning before the verdict is reported to reduce harmful self-preference ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03846), abstract checked). For the +0.44-point effect (analysis §6), add two provenance terms to the version effect: γ_same·[judge is the author's model]·v and γ_fam·[judge is another Claude model]·v. All four authors are Claude models, so γ_fam is confounded with the overall effect level until at least one judge from another family joins the panel. **Adding a non-Claude judge is the single most valuable change to the panel.** The "narcissists" null translates into a check: does the self-excess concentrate on pairs where the other judges disagree, or that auditors flagged as ambiguous? If so, the judge is reading its own manipulation as intended, which is familiarity rather than favouritism, and the fix is better-specified pairs. Leave-own-model-out should stay the default, since it changed no verdict in the pilot (§6). Paraphrasing every vignette into a neutral house style to strip provenance cues is tempting, but it changes the stimulus being scored. It is acceptable only if it is applied identically to both versions and the pair is then re-validated, given the side effects of LLM rewriting described in the next section. No study found measures self-preference in pointwise Likert judgments of paired vignettes, so whether 0.44 points is typical is unknown.

The auditors need the same treatment. "Same 0–3 confound grade about half the time", and 43% exact agreement on originality (pilot audit data), are not reliability statistics. Report exact and adjacent agreement alongside Krippendorff's α (ordinal) and Gwet's AC2 with pre-specified weights. Expect the kappa paradox when most grades fall in one category: chance-corrected agreement can look poor despite high raw agreement ([BMC Medical Research Methodology 2014](https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-14-100), search extract). LLM judges are often inconsistent even with themselves across identical runs, and majority voting over runs helps ([Rating Roulette](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.27106), abstract checked). Dawid–Skene aggregation is a poor fit here. It must estimate 12 free confusion parameters per auditor from about 30 items (computed), and it assumes conditionally independent errors, which LLM judges violate through shared data and failure modes ([arXiv 2601.22336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22336), abstract checked). AGORA instead aggregates judges with a graded-response model built for small evaluation sets ([arXiv 2611.05556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05556), abstract checked). In that model P(grade_{p,j} ≥ k) = logistic(a_j(θ_p − b_{j,k})), where θ_p is the pair's latent confound severity, a_j an auditor's discrimination and b_{j,k} its thresholds. A hand-graded gold subset of 10–15 pairs anchors direction, because shared biases can masquerade as consensus ([LREC 2026 workshop](https://aclanthology.org/2026.cas-1.11.pdf), search extract). Generalizability theory frames an LLM pipeline with prompts, judges and replications as facets, and separates the absolute Φ coefficient from the relative G coefficient ([Hidden measurement error in LLM pipelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11581), abstract checked). The decision study is easy to anticipate: with near-zero repeat variance, budget should buy more judge models and paraphrase facets, not more repeats. Use Φ for "this pair produces an effect" and G for ranking authors. The deeper fix, in the next section, is to stop asking auditors for an unobservable opinion and score their grades against a measured quantity.

Item response theory (IRT) explains why two factors are useless, but it cannot be fitted to four authors. Fluid Benchmarking uses a two-parameter IRT model and selects each next item by maximum Fisher information at the current ability estimate ([fluid-benchmarking](https://github.com/allenai/fluid-benchmarking), read at source); tinyBenchmarks uses IRT to choose small anchor sets ([tinyBenchmarks](https://github.com/felipemaiapolo/tinyBenchmarks), read at source). In that model an item's information is a²P(1 − P), which is largest at P = 0.5 and close to zero when every test-taker succeeds. The intention and knowledge factors, where every author "succeeds", are exactly such items. That is the analysis's zero-entropy result (§1) in IRT language. Jiang and colleagues argue that IRT's assumptions conflict with the data regime of AI evaluation ([Can we trust IRT for AI evaluation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15190), abstract checked). Their reported finding that 30 models are too few to recover item parameters is a search extract that the coordinator did not confirm. Either way, four authors are far too few. Measure a factor's discriminating power instead as the between-author variance of its outcome relative to residual noise, from a variance-components model. A 2026 study of 56 benchmarks across 53 models applied convergent and discriminant validity (not IRT) to ask whether benchmarks measure what they claim ([Desai et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08812), abstract checked). By that standard the prediction score currently fails discriminant validity. A baseline that knows only the factor matches the authors' mean score of 0.82 (§1), so the score tracks textbook knowledge, not design skill. Each scoring run should report the correlation between author scores and the factor baseline.

## "Varies only one factor" is an exclusion restriction readers can test

The pilot's confound audit asked LLMs whether readers would infer something extra from a pair, a question with no operational answer. The causal-inference literature gives it one. Fong and Grimmer revised their own earlier claim: randomising whole texts does not identify the effect of a latent treatment, because other latent features of the same words can confound it ([Causal inference with latent treatments](https://fongsurvey.polisci.lsa.umich.edu/papers/latent-treatments.pdf), search extract). Pryzant and colleagues separate the property a writer intends from the property readers perceive, and readers respond to the perceived value ([Causal effects of linguistic properties](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12919), abstract checked). Dafoe, Zhang and Caughey call the needed condition **information equivalence**. Respondents who learn one attribute revise their beliefs about others, and a valid manipulation must not shift those background beliefs. Formally this is an exclusion restriction that can be checked after the fact with placebo questions ([Political Analysis 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/polals/v26y2018i04p399-416_00.html), search extract). Lin, Morency and Ben-Michael define an "isolated" effect that holds non-focal text fixed ([Isolated causal effects of natural language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14812), abstract checked). A minimal pair meets that definition by construction at the level of the text, but not at the level of what readers infer. So the operational definition is this: **the version affects the judgment only through readers' perceived value of the factor, relative to a pre-declared list of attributes that must not move**. Its observable implication is that the manipulation check differs between versions and the placebo items do not.

Kneer and Machery's Study 3 is a working example in exactly this genre ([No luck for moral luck, Cognition 2019](https://philarchive.org/rec/KNENLF), read at source by the coordinator). In a between-subjects design, 566 participants recruited in the US (after attention and comprehension exclusions) read a bath vignette in which a parent leaves a two-year-old beside a filled bath and the child either drowns or is fine. After the moral judgment, participants answered six belief and probability questions.

| Measure (unlucky vs lucky outcome) | d |
|---|---|
| Wrongness | 0.52 |
| Punishment | 0.76 |
| Four items on what the agent knew or believed about an accident | −0.03 to 0.09 |
| "Should have believed there was a high probability" (negligence) | 0.30 |
| Probability of an accident, 0–100 | 0.41 |

The four knowledge and belief items behaved as placebos. The outcome moved readers' estimate of the ex-ante risk, a variable the text holds fixed. In the authors' bootstrap mediation, negligence was the only significant mediator of wrongness, and probability was a near-complete mediator of outcome on negligence. **A textually perfect moral-luck pair therefore still differs, in readers' minds, in a second variable, and that variable carries the effect.** Whether it counts as a confound depends on the estimand: perceived risk is part of the total effect of the outcome, but not part of a controlled direct effect that holds perceived risk fixed. The study's rating SDs of 1.5–2.0 also support the noise assumption used throughout the analysis.

Three estimands follow, and the benchmark should keep them apart. The first is the average effect of the version, which randomisation always identifies and which authors predict. The second is the effect of the perceived factor. Add a manipulation-check item and form the Wald ratio β_F = (Ȳ_B − Ȳ_A)/(F̄_B − F̄_A). Its exclusion restriction is information equivalence, and the confound ratio κ_p = max_k|ΔU_k|/|ΔF| (standardised placebo shift over manipulation-check shift) is a cheap per-pair index (computed). Imai and Nakamura extend their generative-AI text-as-treatment framework with an instrumental-variables version for features "based on human perception" ([arXiv 2410.00903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.00903), abstract checked; [gpi_pack](https://github.com/gpi-pack/gpi_pack), read at source). The third is the average controlled direct effect, identified by design. Acharya, Blackwell and Sen run a factorial arm that states the suspect attribute in both versions; the "eliminated effect", the total effect minus the controlled direct effect, measures how much of the gap depends on that inference ([Political Analysis 2018](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cup:polals:v:26:y:2018:i:04:p:357-378_00), search extract). For moral-luck pairs, the natural arm states that the probability of harm was the same and known in both versions. Two caveats apply. The controlled direct effect is not identified if the stated value fails to fix readers' beliefs, so the arm needs its own belief item. And added contextual detail tends to dampen treatment effects ([Brutger et al., AJPS 2023](https://dtingley.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/abstraction-and-detail-experimental-design), search extract), so these arms must not be scored against the author's prediction for the original pair. Measured-mediator analysis of the Kneer–Machery kind is descriptive at best. Blackwell, Ma and Opacic show that showing a treatment moved a mediator can neither establish nor rule out a mechanism without a rarely stated monotonicity assumption ([Assumption smuggling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07072), abstract checked).

Applying this means writing per-factor lists before authors write any pairs. Each factor needs three lists. Allowed mediators are part of what the factor means. Contested mediators define the philosophical question; for moral luck these are perceived probability, foreseeability and negligence. Placebo attributes are things that should not move. Some reader inferences are rational consequences of the manipulation: norm violations are reported to inform readers' ascriptions of intention ([Uttich & Lombrozo](https://cognition.princeton.edu/document/237), search extract), and "is X a cause?" can be read as a question about accountability ([Samland & Waldmann](https://www.psych.uni-goettingen.de/de/cognition/publications/samlandwaldmann2016), search extract). The norm-and-causation factor therefore needs a question-interpretation probe as well as placebo items. Dafoe and colleagues' appendix shows how to do the rest ([online appendix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yaleirsurveys/information_equivalence/master/IE_online_only_appendix.pdf), read at source). They chose placebos systematically by ranking 114 real-world attributes by how much they co-vary with the manipulated one, and asked about the world "a decade ago" so the scenario could not shape the answer. Placebo questions placed before the outcome changed neither the outcome nor the treatment effect, and telling respondents that the situation was general and not about any specific country left the imbalance unchanged. The benchmark should therefore never tell readers to "consider only what is stated". The same appendix found that only 5 of 35 vignette articles in top political-science journals even mentioned the issue (read at source). Two post-treatment rules apply. Never drop or subset readers by manipulation-check answers ([Montgomery, Nyhan & Torres](https://sites.dartmouth.edu/nyhan/files/2021/03/post-treatment-bias.pdf), search extract). The survey's own rule drops people who answer fewer than 80% of the per-scenario factual questions correctly ([survey plan](../docs/human-survey.md), read at source), and those questions come after each vignette. The plan's promise to report results without exclusions is the safeguard, and making that analysis primary would be safer still. Placebo tests have little power: detecting a placebo shift of d = 0.5 needs about 63 readers per version (computed). Report equivalence bounds rather than "no significant difference", and consider extra, cheaper readers who answer only the placebo items.

LLM readers are a screen, not a measurement. Gui and Toubia show that changing the treatment in a prompt shifts an LLM's implicit assumptions about variables the prompt leaves unspecified ([arXiv 2312.15524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15524), abstract checked). Lin and colleagues show that interventions in LLM-simulated experiments shift latent attributes of the simulated population, which can inflate or attenuate effects ([The illusion of intervention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20767), abstract checked). Their diagnostic, total-variation distance between versions on negative-control outcomes, is a search extract. That diagnostic ports directly. Have judge models from several families answer the frozen placebo battery for each version, and report per-item total-variation distance. Whether an LLM's placebo imbalance predicts the human imbalance for these factors is untested, and the human run is the place to measure it.

Text-side checks are necessary but cannot be sufficient, because the minimal edit is itself what triggers the extra inferences. A deterministic gate (differing spans, edit distance outside the manipulated span, length and entity parity) catches gross asymmetries reproducibly, unlike a 0–3 opinion grade. LLM rewriting is not a reliable fix. Generative-model interventions have "considerable undesired side effects" on unrelated content ([Ravfogel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07180), abstract checked). Text distillation trades bias reduction against losing real confounders ([Daoud et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02400), abstract checked). Residual attribute signal is reported to remain detectable after LLM "disentanglement" (search extract via the same paper). Auditors can be calibrated the way NLP calibrates explanation methods. Neutral perturbations applied identically to both versions should leave a confound grade unchanged, which is counterfactual invariance ([Veitch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00545), abstract checked). Planted single-concept edits of graded strength, on the model of the CEBaB benchmark ([Abraham et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14140), abstract checked), measure each auditor's recall by confound type. The auditor grade then becomes a prediction of measured placebo imbalance, and its quality is a correlation or AUC against that measurement rather than agreement with other auditors. Design-based supervised learning on a random subsample of pairs with known probabilities turns the cheap grades into unbiased benchmark-level confound rates.

Two design problems sit underneath all of this. With one pair per factor and author, any oddity of the pair is inseparable from the factor. Wells and Windschitl's stimulus-sampling argument and Westfall, Judd and Kenny's crossed random effects are the remedy ([Wells & Windschitl 1999](https://www2.psych.ubc.ca/~schaller/528Readings/WellsWindschitl1999.pdf), search extract). Ask authors for several re-skinned pairs per factor; variation in effects across re-skins is then a behavioural confound signal. And each participant sees both levels of almost every factor (analysis §8). Kneer and Machery report that when the same participants saw both outcomes, most judged the lucky and unlucky agents equally blameworthy and wrong, with punishment more outcome-dependent (search extract, from the article record). The survey records item order, so test whether a pair's gap differs after the participant has met the opposite side of its factor. Finally, Egami and colleagues' rule from text-as-data work applies to confound discovery: anything discovered by searching the data must be confirmed on fresh data ([How to make causal inferences using texts](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02163), abstract checked; the `texteffect` documentation warns the training-set posterior is "inappropriate" for causal inference, [sibp.Rd](https://github.com/cran/texteffect/blob/master/man/sibp.Rd), read at source). Placebo lists must be frozen before the confirmatory run and not re-tuned on its data.

## Proper scores, planned looks and three-way verdicts replace the binned rule

The current prediction score gives half credit for direction and half for size, with adjacent bins earning half the size credit ([score.py](../score.py), read at source). A single category report cannot be strictly proper. A proper scoring rule rewards a forecaster for reporting their true belief, and this rule never asks for one ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf), search extract). The analysis showed that the best report differs from the most likely outcome in about one case in five and pulls towards "small" (§2). The researchers' worked case makes the point vividly (computed). An author with beliefs (0, 0, 0.4, 0.3, 0.3) over B-large, B-small, none, A-small and A-large should report "A-small", which is worth 0.625 in expectation against 0.475 for "none", even though "none" is the most likely outcome. Theory explains why patching the matrix will not help. Squared error elicits the mean, absolute error the median, and quantiles are elicitable ([Gneiting 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.0902), abstract checked), but the mode of a continuous distribution is not elicitable by any scoring function ([Heinrich](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08123), abstract checked). To learn what an author thinks most likely, ask for probabilities.

The replacement has three parts. First, elicit a probability vector over the five ordered bins and score it with the **ranked probability score**: RPS = Σ_k (P_k − O_k)² over the cumulative forecast P_k and cumulative outcome O_k, optionally divided by K − 1. Add a log score floored at a small ε as a secondary score. RPS is a sum of Brier scores on the nested events "gap ≤ bin k", each uniquely minimised at the true cumulative probability, so it is strictly proper for a distribution over ordered bins (computed argument; one source calls it proper but not strictly, search extract). Second, elicit a point gap with an 80% interval, or 10/50/90% quantiles. Score them with absolute error, pinball loss and the interval score IS = (u − l) + (2/α)(l − y)·1{y < l} + (2/α)(y − u)·1{y > u}, or with the closed-form CRPS for a Gaussian forecast (standard forms; R implementations in [scoringRules](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04743), abstract checked). Third, handle outcome noise explicitly. Ferro argues that proper scores computed against noisy verification data favour good forecasts of the observation rather than of the truth, and he constructs error-corrected scores ([Ferro 2017, QJRMS](https://ore.exeter.ac.uk/repository/handle/10871/28275), search extract). The simplest honest route is to score against the observable. Widen an author's forecast N(μ, σ_f²) for the true gap to N(μ, σ_f² + s_p²) for the observed one, where s_p is the pair's realised standard error, then apply CRPS or the log score; that stays proper for the observable (computed). As the analysis notes, squared error has the convenient property that noise adds the same constant s_p² to every forecast's expected loss, so the ranking of authors is preserved in expectation (§2). The current rule can survive as a descriptive hit rate, always reported next to its oracle ceiling. The analysis showed that with 8 people per version, an author who knows the true gap exactly expects only about 0.5 on a true 1-point gap (§3).

Scores mean little without baselines. Hewitt and colleagues compared LLM forecasts with zero-effect and "mean of other studies" baselines (search extract, same source as above). In the first round of a replicability-prediction challenge, every team beat random scores but every team underperformed a constant-0.5 Brier baseline ([Center for Open Science](https://www.cos.io/blog/predicting-replicability-challenge-round-1-results-round-2-opportunity), search extract). That is the analysis's §1 finding in another field: a factor-only baseline scores 0.82, the same as the authors. Report a skill score, the author's score minus a leave-author-out factor baseline's score, and expect it to be near zero on the current factors. The researchers found no 2024–2026 benchmark that scores LLM effect-size forecasts with CRPS or interval scores, or corrects the score for outcome noise. The benchmark would be early here, so its scoring rules should be fixed and published before results. The analysis's target-size and adjudication tasks (§1) fit naturally into this framework: a target-size task is an interval or squared-error score against a designated gap.

Looking at results as people arrive turns a 6% false-gap rate into 24% if the classification rule is applied at each look (§5). Safe, anytime-valid inference rests on test martingales, e-processes and confidence sequences that hold at every sample size ([Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk & Shafer, Statistical Science 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01948), abstract checked). The `confseq` package implements mixture boundaries and Waudby-Smith and Ramdas's betting confidence sequences for bounded means ([confseq](https://github.com/gostevehoward/confseq), read at source; [betting paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686), abstract checked). The survey's mirror forms make a nonasymptotic construction possible (computed). Consecutive participants see opposite versions of every pair, so each completed pair of participants yields one paired difference in [−6, 6] per vignette pair, which can be rescaled to [0, 1] and fed to a betting sequence. A participant who drops out orphans their partner, so the construction depends on forms being handed out in order. Simulated half-widths in rating points show the price (computed):

| Participants per version | Fixed-n t-interval | Gaussian-mixture sequence | Hedged-capital betting sequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 1.79 | 2.36 | 3.35 |
| 20 | 1.02 | 1.47 | 1.88 |
| 40 | 0.70 | 1.06 | 1.13 |

With 16–40 people in total, a single pair's anytime-valid interval rarely excludes zero unless the gap is about 2 points or more. Spend the sequential machinery on aggregates. For the mean over 30 pairs of predicted sign × gap, the anytime-valid half-width is roughly 0.4–0.5 points at 8 per version (computed). Classify individual pairs only at one pre-set sample size, or at two or three pre-registered looks with group-sequential error spending. A 2026 note shows that classical combination tests from adaptive designs are formally equivalent to sequential e-value tests ([arXiv 2606.00878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00878), abstract checked). Lindon and colleagues give anytime-valid confidence sequences for regression coefficients, so the participant-adjusted model can be monitored too ([arXiv 2210.08589](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08589), abstract checked). `safestats` provides safe t-tests that survive optional stopping ([safestats](https://github.com/AlexanderLyNL/safestats), read at source). One fixed-n fix applies regardless. At 8 per version the percentile bootstrap uses roughly z-quantiles and slightly underestimates the standard error, giving a half-width of 1.47 against a t-based 1.61 (computed), so a Welch t-interval is the honest choice.

"No difference" must be a positive finding, not the absence of one. Two one-sided tests conclude equivalence when the 90% interval lies inside pre-set bounds ([Lakens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906), search extract). Kruschke's region-of-practical-equivalence rule accepts a null when the posterior interval sits inside the region and otherwise stays undecided ([Kruschke 2018](https://jjcurtin.github.io/book_iaml/pdfs/kruschke2018a.pdf), search extract). The arithmetic is unforgiving (computed). With SD 1.5 and bounds of ±0.5, equivalence becomes possible at all only from about 49 people per version, and then only if the observed gap is exactly zero. 80% power at a true zero needs about 154 per version, or roughly 110–125 with a participant intercept. The project's `equivalence_supported` flag, a 95% interval inside ±0.5 (read at source), is stricter still. Each pair should therefore receive a three-way verdict: confirmed in a direction, equivalent, or **inconclusive**. At this survey's sizes most small gaps belong in the third category. "None" forecasts should be scored by the probabilistic rules above, never credited merely because |d| < 0.5: at 8 per version, a true gap of 0.75 lands in "none" 32% of the time (computed). The null controls should be tested for equivalence, as the analysis proposes (§4). Fixing the 0.5 margin in advance, as the analysis plan already does, avoids the post-hoc-margin problem studied by a 2026 sequential-equivalence paper ([arXiv 2603.16213](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16213), search extract).

Thirty pairs at once raise both a multiplicity problem and a shrinkage opportunity. Benjamini–Hochberg at q = 0.10 on fixed-n p-values at the final look is the default. It is valid under positive dependence, which ratings from the same people across pairs plausibly induce but nobody has verified; Benjamini–Yekutieli is the fallback. e-BH controls the false discovery rate under arbitrary dependence ([Wang & Ramdas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02824), abstract checked). With 30 pairs it needs an e-value of at least 300/j for the j-th discovery, which at 8 per version means mean gaps of about 2.3–3 points (computed), so it is useful only with continuously monitored e-processes. For sizes, replace raw gaps with partially pooled ones: d_j ~ N(δ_j, s_j²), δ_j = μ_f + β·m_j + η_j, with η_j ~ N(0, τ²) and the panel gap m_j as a covariate. Fit it with full Bayes and half-normal priors, because a method-of-moments τ from 30 pairs is very noisy, and report each pair's posterior probability of every bin. Robust empirical-Bayes intervals keep average coverage whatever the distribution of true effects ([Armstrong, Kolesár & Plagborg-Møller](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.03448), abstract checked). A 2026 *Econometrica* paper shows how to shrink when estimation precision predicts the parameter ([Chen, CLOSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.14444), abstract checked); that applies here if pairs with fewer completed ratings differ systematically. Van Zwet and colleagues estimate that just-significant trial estimates exaggerate effects by about 1.7× ([Statistics in Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9290572/), search extract). The analogue here is that observed "large" gaps are inflated, so the plan to re-set the size bins on human data should use shrunken gaps. Forecasts should be scored against raw gaps with noise-aware scores, or by expected RPS under the posterior, and never against shrunken point estimates, which reward forecasts that sit near the prior (computed).

Ranking authors is a question about pairs, not people. The analysis found that author differences as large as those observed arise by chance 38% of the time, and that detecting a 0.2 difference in mean score needs about 26 pairs per author (§7). Westfall, Kenny and Judd show that when stimuli are a random sample, power levels off as participants increase, at a ceiling set by the number of stimuli ([JEP: General 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111580), search extract). An author's mean over five pairs has a standard error of at least SD_pairs/√5, however many people take part. Decide which estimand is wanted. "How well did this author do on these pairs" needs only human precision. "How good is this author at designing thought experiments" needs more pairs per author, on fewer and more informative factors.

## Originality belongs to the pool and is measured on structure

The Vendi score is the exponential of the entropy of the eigenvalues of K/n, where K is a similarity matrix with unit diagonal. Its order-q generalisation ranges from counting distinct items (small q) to weighting the dominant cluster (large q) ([Vendi-Score README](https://github.com/vertaix/Vendi-Score), read at source; [Pasarkar & Dieng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12952), abstract checked). If K is categorical, with K_ij = 1 when two pairs fall in the same class and 0 otherwise, the eigenvalues of K/n are exactly the class proportions. The Vendi score then *is* the Hill number of the class distribution (computed). That makes the analysis's §10 figures (effective numbers 14.8 at order 1 and 13.3 at order 2 for 20 pairs, and leave-one-out contributions per author) a special case of the mainstream measure. Two cautions come with the method. The kernel dominates the answer: on the README's own five-sentence example, n-gram, BERT and SimCSE kernels give 3.91, 1.21 and 2.81 (read at source). And diversity estimates depend on the sample. Farnia, Jalali and Ospanov analyse the statistical origin of the bias between generated-sample and test-sample diversity ([arXiv 2602.14682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14682), abstract checked). Their finding that the expected score rises with sample size is a search extract, but it agrees with the analysis's own caution that only equal-size sets can be compared.

The convergence evidence is real but fragile. The "Artificial Hivemind" paper reports strong homogeneity across language models on open-ended prompts ([Jiang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22954), abstract checked). Schaeffer and colleagues' 2026 re-examination finds that the flagship example does not show the claimed two-cluster collapse ([arXiv 2609.33936](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33936), abstract checked). They also report, per search extracts, three further points: the 0.8 cosine threshold lies far in the tail of the original null, cosine similarity is only a modest predictor of whether two responses express the same idea, and a harder null shrinks the convergence without erasing it. Wenger and Kenett find that individual LLM responses are as original as human ones while population-level variability is much lower, and lower still within one model family ([We're different, we're the same](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361), abstract checked). Patel and colleagues report cross-model diversity falling over three years of models ([arXiv 2608.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19437), abstract checked). NoveltyBench partitions generations into functional-equivalence classes and finds frontier models lack distributional diversity ([arXiv 2504.05228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05228), abstract checked). Four Claude authors with 8 of 20 pairs sharing a setting is therefore what this literature predicts. Without a reference collision rate from other model families or human authors, though, any threshold for "too homogeneous" is arbitrary.

A protocol that survives the re-analysis has four parts. Define "same setting" and "same device" operationally. Hand-label all 190 pairings among 20 items for both relations as gold. Calibrate any automatic judge against that gold and report its precision and recall, rather than borrowing the 0.8 cosine cut-off that research-idea deduplication used ([AI-Researcher repository](https://github.com/NoviScl/AI-Researcher), read at source). And use a hard null: similarity between items the gold labels as different ideas for the same factor. To separate a model's repetition of itself from convergence between models, sample 5–10 pairs per model per factor; with one sample per cell the two are confounded, and one coincidence swings a model's score. Two standard ecology estimators turn collisions into set-level numbers: the unbiased Simpson effective number n(n − 1)/Σm_k(m_k − 1), and Chao1 = S_obs + f₁²/(2f₂), a lower bound on the number of settings the pooled generators would eventually produce. On the project's own setting annotations (13 singletons and clusters of 2, 2 and 3 among the 20 authored pairs) they give about 38 for Simpson and 42 to 58 for Chao1, depending on the bias correction (computed here). Both sit far above the 16 settings observed, and the spread between them shows how little 20 items reveal about the tail.

Raw-text similarity rewards exactly the re-skinning the benchmark should penalise, so representation matters more than metric. Rewrite each pair into a fixed schema with names and places stripped. The schema records the factor, the manipulated variable, the contrast device, and the causal structure (means versus side effect, avoidable versus inevitable harm, action versus omission, following OffTheRails templates, [arXiv 2404.10975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10975), abstract checked), with the setting kept as a separate field. Similarity on the structural fields then measures re-skinning; similarity on the setting field measures homogenisation. The SemEval-2026 narrative-similarity task defines similarity by theme, course of action and outcome ([arXiv 2604.21782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21782), abstract checked), and systems that first converted stories into structured descriptions reportedly did well (search extract). LLMs are weak at recognising far analogies zero-shot ([ARN, Sourati et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00996), abstract checked), which is another reason to judge abstractions rather than raw text. Re-skins of famous cases call for a retrieve-then-judge design. Build a reference corpus from MoCa's factor-annotated stories drawn from 24 cognitive-science papers ([arXiv 2310.19677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.19677), abstract checked), plus hand-entered canonical cases. Then retrieve the nearest cases and ask a narrow same-device question. This design is how rephrased benchmark items that evade n-gram checks are caught ([arXiv 2311.04850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04850), abstract checked). Two behavioural tests add evidence. A recognition test asks readers which published case a pair is a version of, multiple-choice with position controls, after the Data Contamination Quiz ([arXiv 2311.06233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06233), abstract checked); the survey's closing question on whether any scenario seemed familiar ([survey plan](../docs/human-survey.md), read at source) is a crude version that could ask which. An echo test counts how many default generations from other models share a pair's device, after the Sui Generis score for plots ([Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00273), abstract checked), giving originality −log((e + 1)/(N + 1)) (computed form). The Creativity Index catches only verbatim or near-verbatim reuse, against corpora with 2020–2023 cutoffs ([creativity_index](https://github.com/GXimingLu/creativity_index), read at source), so it is low priority for conceptual re-skins. Holistic LLM originality grades should be dropped: LLM judges and non-experts reportedly rated LLM stories more creative than experts did ([Ismayilzada et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02316), search extract). Use LLM calls only for narrow classifications whose accuracy can be checked against hand labels.

How originality is scored changes what authors do. In Raghavan's Scattergories model, where a player scores only for a unique answer, competition pushes producers towards diversity but the equilibrium remains less diverse than is socially optimal. A model's standing in isolation can also differ from its standing against competitors ([Competition and diversity in generative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08610), abstract checked). Scores relative to peers therefore shift whenever the line-up changes and cannot be compared across runs. The headline score should be novelty against a fixed, versioned reference pool of canonical cases plus frozen default generations, with convergence against peers reported as a secondary statistic. Uniqueness alone rewards incoherence, so originality must be gated by validity: only pairs that move judgments as intended, without a measured confound, enter the pool. Diversity-aware training rewards multiply quality by a semantic-diversity term ([DARLING](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02534), abstract checked), which is the right shape for a benchmark score. Two more steps complete the scoring. Normalise within factor against the reference distribution of devices, because knowledge pairs have very few known devices. And run the brief with and without an explicit originality instruction, to see whether collisions fall or merely move to the second-most-obvious idea.

## A two-phase pipeline puts each method where the data allow

Several gaps run through the literature and decide what the benchmark must measure for itself. No study found tests whether LLM placebo imbalances predict human ones for moral or epistemic vignettes. None measures self-preference in pointwise Likert judgments of paired vignettes. None gives a formal guarantee that an LLM-generated minimal pair changes only one reader-perceived attribute, and none handles ordinal 1–7 labels with a near-deterministic judge inside PPI. There is no human baseline for how often independent authors collide on setting or device when writing such pairs. No published convention exists for equivalence margins on placebo items; the 0.3 SD the researchers suggest is a placeholder. And the participant intraclass correlation for these ratings is unknown, with 0.16–0.28 used only for illustration. The first human run can supply several of these numbers. That is a stronger reason to run it as a calibration study than any pair-level verdict it could deliver.

## Conclusion

The common thread is that every cheap measurement in the benchmark is a surrogate, and the same design logic disciplines all of them. Panel gaps predict human gaps. Auditor grades predict measured placebo imbalance. Originality judgments predict a hand-labelled partition. Authors' forecasts predict observed gaps. In each case the recent statistics says the same thing: pick a human-measured target, sample it with known probabilities, score the surrogate against it, and let the surrogate buy precision only through its measured correlation. Framed this way, the benchmark's open questions become quantities with standard errors: τ, γ, the slope, a factor's between-author variance, an auditor's AUC.

The research also shows where cleverness stops paying. No estimator overcomes thirty pairs, five per author, or a judge family shared with the authors. Several "fixes" change the very stimulus being scored: stating background details, provenance-stripping paraphrase, telling readers to consider the case abstractly. They are useful only as separate estimands. The analysis's §1 finding that the score now measures textbook knowledge is therefore not a statistical problem to be corrected downstream. It is a design signal, and the recent literature points the same way: retarget factors so success is uncertain, score skill against a baseline, and spend the budget on more distinct pairs and a second judge family rather than on more repeats or more readers per pair.
